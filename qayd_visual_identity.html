§1 The mark

One monogram, read in two scripts

The mark is a single monoline glyph that a Latin reader sees as q and an Arabic reader sees as ق (qaf) — the first sound of qayd in both scripts.

A circular bowl with a descending tail carries the “q”. The two dots set over its right shoulder are the qaf’s signature — kept off-centre so the mark never reads as a face. The dots become a quiet recurring device throughout this guide.

Why dual-script: a bilingual audience should recognise itself in the mark at first glance (cultural match & recognition). One glyph for both scripts keeps the brand single and memorable instead of splitting into two logos.

Clear space & minimum size

Keep clear space on every side equal to the height of the two dots. The glyph stays legible down to 24 px; the tiled icon down to 44 px (a comfortable touch target). Don’t recolour the glyph outside the palette, add effects, rotate it, or stretch the tile.

§2 Color — Academic Indigo

A calm, studious palette that earns its contrast

Indigo is the scholarly anchor; a single teal accent marks what is live or complete. Everything else is a cool, quiet neutral so content leads and the interface recedes.

Every text and interface pair below was checked with a WCAG contrast script (scheme_check.py) — all meet AA, most reach AAA, in both light and dark. Use the Dark toggle to preview the dark scheme live.

§3 Typography

IBM Plex Sans & IBM Plex Sans Arabic

One humanist superfamily carries both scripts. Open apertures and even rhythm keep long transcripts and summaries easy on the eye; the Arabic companion shares the same proportions, so a bilingual screen never looks stitched together.

Fallbacks: Noto Sans / Noto Sans Arabic, then the platform sans. No separate display face — hierarchy comes from size and weight, not from a second personality.

Why one family across scripts: consistent letterforms mean a student’s eye learns the interface once. The layout mirrors to right-to-left automatically, so switching language is a flip — not a redesign — honouring “match between system and the real world”.

§4 Shape & space

Soft corners, low shadows, room to breathe

Spacing steps in multiples of 8 px (4 px for fine tuning). Corners are gently rounded; elevation stays shallow so the screen feels calm rather than busy.

Why shallow elevation: a study tool should feel quiet. Low shadows and wide margins reduce visual noise, so a long transcript stays the focus and nothing on screen shouts.

§5 Iconography

Material Symbols, rounded

Line icons with rounded terminals echo the monogram’s geometry. One weight, 24 px on a 44 px touch target, always paired with a text label for the core actions.

§7 Motion

Movement that answers an action

Motion confirms what a tap did — a sheet opening, a line highlighting as the audio plays — rather than decorating the screen. One gentle reveal when a list first loads; nothing loops or drifts on its own.

TokenDurationEasingUsed for
short150 msstandardButton press, chip select, ripple
medium250 msemphasizedSheets, dialogs, screen transitions
reveal400 msdecelerateList load — the one orchestrated moment

Why so little motion: students often open the app in a lecture hall or late at night. Calm, purposeful movement respects attention and battery, and prefers-reduced-motion switches it off entirely.

§8 Voice & tone

Plain, calm, and specific

qayd speaks like a helpful study partner: short sentences, active verbs, no jargon and no hype. Buttons say exactly what happens; errors say what went wrong and what to do next.

MomentWe sayNot
Primary action“Record lecture”“Start” / “Submit”
While working“Summarizing in English…”“Please wait”
Empty list“No Qayds yet — record or import a lecture.”“Nothing here”
Error“Couldn’t reach the service. Your recording is safe — try again.”“Error 503” / “Oops!”
Done“Summary ready.”“Success!”
Why this tone: clear, non-blaming words lower anxiety at exactly the moments software usually raises it — waiting and failing — and keep the one action name consistent from tap to confirmation so the product stays easy to learn.
